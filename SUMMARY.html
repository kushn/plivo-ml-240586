The result

Two corrections this log records rather than hides. The first pipeline reported 100 ms at AUC 1.000 — that was the model scored on its own training turns. And the silence-only baseline is not 1600 ms on both folders: run it per-folder and it scores 1600 ms on english but 850 ms on hindi, because fewer than 5% of hindi turns contain a hold longer than 850 ms, so a dumb timer already fits the budget. An earlier draft of this page claimed −47% on hindi against a 1600 ms reference. That was wrong: on hindi this model ties the timer.

How it works

For a pause at pause_start, the first statement of extract_features is cut = x[:int(pause_start*sr)]. Nothing downstream ever sees a later sample, so pause_end — future information for a hold — cannot leak into a feature. The 60 columns are four families:

  • pitch YIN F0 in semitones re 100 Hz, slope over the last 500 ms and 1.5 s, final value against a 3 s local speaker reference, range, voiced fraction, creak fraction.
  • energy RMS(dB) decay rate into the pause, drop across the final 300 ms, level relative to the turn's own 90th percentile.
  • timing final voiced-run duration against the window mean (pre-boundary lengthening), syllable-rate proxy, elapsed time, pause index.
  • spectral trailing MFCC mean/std/delta over the last 500 ms.

Voicing is decided by an energy gate rather than pYIN's Viterbi decode: frames must sit within 25 dB of the window peak, off the fmin/fmax rails, and survive a 50 ms median filter. That swap cut extraction from minutes to 22 ms per pause, which is what made the ablation below affordable.

The finding that mattered

The terminal pitch cue is discriminative in both languages with opposite sign. Hindi follows the textbook prior — turn ends fall (−2.60 st/s) while holds stay level (+0.22). This English corpus inverts it: ends rise (+1.97) and holds fall (−3.76). One global "falling pitch means done" rule is therefore wrong half the time, which explains the worst cell in the ablation (train hindi → test english, 1393 ms) and predicts the next experiment: give the model an explicit language/channel signal instead of making it infer one.

Two planned changes did not survive the data. Pruning the MFCC block hurt (0.705 → 0.685 → 0.650): those columns are not noise, they act as an implicit language/channel identifier that lets the trees condition the sign of the prosodic rule above. Capacity reduction bought nothing in score (1079 → 1078 ms); it was kept because it makes the in-sample numbers honest and trains 3× faster, not because it wins points. A fifth idea — weighting training rows by hold duration, since the scorer only charges a false cutoff when the delay elapses before the user resumes — moved the metric 16 ms, one grid step, and was rejected as noise.

What finally moved the metric

The scorer charges a false cutoff only when the action delay elapses before the user resumes, so firing on a 200 ms hesitation is free — yet short holds are ~80% of the negatives. Reweighting training rows by what a mistake on them actually costs (long holds ×3, short holds ×0.15) and swapping HistGradientBoosting for ExtraTrees took english from 1168 to 1130 ms. pause_end is future information at inference and is never a feature; it is used only to weight training rows, which is label information.

Where the model stands

Why this beats the status quo

The silence-only baseline is a fixed timer, and its cost depends entirely on how long the language's mid-turn hesitations run. On english the timer must wait 1600 ms to stay inside the 5% interruption budget; this model reads how the speaker arrived at the silence — terminal pitch movement, energy decay shape, final-syllable lengthening, position in the turn — and commits at 600 ms when the prosody is unambiguous, for 1130 ms mean delay at the same budget. On hindi the timer is already at 850 ms and the model matches it but does not beat it.

The reason is visible in the data rather than the model: at a 700 ms action delay only 12 hindi turns contain a hold long enough to be cuttable, and separating true ends from those twelve is a 0.68-AUC problem for the current features. There is no threshold that fires earlier without spending more than five turns. Saying so is more useful than a number that would not reproduce on the hidden set — and it names the target precisely: to beat 850 ms, the model must rank turn ends above roughly a dozen specific long holds.

The shipped scores are floored at the scorer's lowest swept threshold (0.05). Compressing into [0.05, 1] changes no ranking, but keeps the always-fire operating point reachable, so the timer becomes a floor the model cannot fall below — without it, an early hindi configuration scored 857 ms, worse than doing nothing.
